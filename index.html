The short version

Your save file stays on your phone. We run no servers, and nothing you do in the game — your keeper’s name, your progress, your results — is ever sent to us. We could not read it if we wanted to.

The one exception is advertising. The game shows ads from Google AdMob, and fetching an ad means Google’s servers see your device: its advertising ID, its IP address, and basic model information. That happens between your phone and Google. It does not pass through us, and it carries nothing from your save.

We wrote the long version anyway, because Google Play requires one and because a policy that only says “we respect your privacy” tells you nothing.

What the app stores on your device

All of it lives in the app’s private storage area, which only this app can read.

What the app does not do

These are statements of fact about the build you are running, not promises about intent:

  • We have no servers, and the app never contacts us. The INTERNET permission exists for one reason — the ad SDK described below — and the app itself contains no HTTP client, no socket, and no websocket code of its own. There is no address belonging to us for anything to be sent to.
  • Your save is never uploaded. Not to us, not to Google, not to the ad network. Your keeper’s name, your career, your statistics, and your inventory stay in the app’s private storage on the phone, and no part of them is attached to an ad request.
  • No analytics or telemetry. No usage statistics, crash reports, or event logs are collected — by us or by anyone through this app.
  • No account, no sign-in. The app does not ask who you are.
  • No purchases. There is no real-money billing. Coins and gems are earned in the game and have no monetary value.
  • No location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos, or files. The app requests none of these permissions.
  • No ad that starts on its own inside a match. Every ad that grants you something is one you chose by pressing a button that said so first.

Advertising

The game is free, and ads are how it pays for itself. They come from Google AdMob. Google is an independent controller of the data it collects this way, not a processor acting for us — which means Google’s own privacy policy governs it, and we have no access to it.

What an ad request involves. To choose and deliver an ad, the SDK sends Google your device’s advertising ID (AAID), an app-set ID that identifies this app on this device, IP address, device model, operating system version, screen size, coarse region derived from the IP address, and which ad slot in the game was shown. It does not send your keeper’s name, your save file, or anything you typed. All of it travels encrypted.

Where ads appear. In five places you choose to enter — extra gems, a heart refill, a second chance at a saved shot, the keeper’s instinct, and changing your keeper’s name — plus one that appears on its own: after the results screen of every third match, and never after a defeat. That is the complete list.

Your consent, and how to change your mind. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland the app asks before any personalised ad is requested, using Google’s certified consent platform (UMP). Your answer is remembered. To change it later, open Settings → Ad privacy options inside the game. Where the law does not require the question, the form is not shown.

Opting out on the device itself. Android lets you delete your advertising ID entirely: Settings → Privacy → Ads. Ads still appear, but they stop being personalised, and the ID is no longer available to any app on the phone.

Google’s policy for this data: policies.google.com/technologies/ads

Device information

When you open the developer diagnostic screen, the app reads your device model, operating system version, graphics adapter name, screen size, and screen density, and displays them to you on that screen. These values are used to choose the right layout and are never stored and never transmitted. They exist only for as long as the screen is open.

Children

Be a Goalkeeper is not directed at children, and it is not listed in Google Play’s Families programme. Its store listing declares a general audience.

The only thing a player types is a goalkeeper’s name, and that name stays in the app’s private storage on the phone — we never receive it and cannot read it.

Because the game does show ads, we say plainly what that means rather than claiming an exemption we no longer have: an ad request reaches Google whoever is holding the phone. If you are a parent and would rather your child’s device did not carry an advertising ID at all, Android can delete it — Settings → Privacy → Ads — and it applies to every app, not only this one.

We do not knowingly collect anything from a child, under GDPR Article 8, COPPA, or Indonesia’s Personal Data Protection Law (UU 27/2022); we hold no data about any player at all.

Your rights, and how to exercise them

Because your data never leaves your device, you do not need to ask us for it.

  • Access it: it is in the app, on the screens that show it.
  • Delete it: uninstalling the app, or clearing its data in Android Settings → Apps → Be a Goalkeeper → Storage → Clear data, erases everything permanently. We cannot recover it, because we never had it.
  • Take it elsewhere: the save is a JSON file in the app’s private storage.

We hold nothing about you, so there is nothing for us to correct, export, or delete on your behalf.

Data sharing

We hold nothing, so we have nothing to sell, rent, or disclose, and we do none of those things.

There is exactly one recipient to list, and it never receives anything from us: Google, which receives an ad request directly from your phone when an ad is shown, as described under Advertising.

Changes to this policy

This policy describes the app as it is built today. If a future version adds analytics, accounts, cloud saves, or in-app purchases, this policy will be updated in the same release that adds them, the effective date will change, and the app will tell you inside Settings before the new behaviour begins. We will not quietly widen what we collect.

The 5 September 2026 revision is that promise being kept: advertising was listed in the previous version as planned and absent, and this version was published in the same release that added it.

Still planned and still not present: Google sign-in with cloud saved games, and in-app purchases. Neither is in the app today.
